What you can make here

Paper. Lined paper in the three US school rulings – wide (11/32 in), college (9/32 in) and narrow (1/4 in) – or any custom spacing from 3 to 20 mm. Graph paper in metric and imperial grids with bold lines every 2, 4, 5 or 10 squares. Dot grid from 4 mm to 1 cm in three dot sizes, and isometric paper with 60° triangles as lines or dots. A print test page shows whether your printer keeps the scale.

Calendars. A monthly calendar for any month from 1900 to 2100 – or all twelve in one PDF – with ISO week numbers and a notes area, and a year at a glance on a single sheet. Both start the week on Monday or Sunday and can print month and weekday names in ten languages, as can the weekly planner and the habit tracker.

Planners. A weekly planner, dated or undated, with a to-do column; a daily planner with an hourly or half-hourly schedule you can set from 4:00 to midnight; and a meal planner with a grocery list sorted into five groups.

Trackers and lists. A habit tracker that prints your own habits, a checklist maker with up to 60 lines and optional pre-filled items, and a budget planner with planned, actual and difference columns for your own categories.

Made for real printers

Every template is built in the paper size you choose – A4, US Letter, A5 or Half Letter – so nothing has to be shrunk to fit. Grids, boxes and text stay at least 12 mm from the edge (9 mm on the two small sizes), more than the unprintable border of typical home printers; only the lines of lined paper deliberately run from edge to edge, and the small optional site credit sits in the bottom margin. The Print button sends the exact page size to your browser with no extra margins, and the PDF stores it too. Scale is the one setting to watch: print at 100% or “Actual size”, as our guide to printing at actual size explains, and a 5 mm grid really measures 5 mm. To check your own printer, print the test page with its 100 mm square and edge rulers; the paper size reference calculates what any “Fit” setting does to a page.
