Meal planner

Plan the week's meals in one grid and write the shopping list right next to it – sorted by category so you shop faster.

Why plan meals?

Deciding what to eat every evening takes surprising energy. Planning the week in one go means one shopping trip, less food thrown away and fewer last-minute takeaways. It also makes it easier to use up leftovers on purpose.

A 20-minute planning routine

  1. Check the calendar: busy evenings get quick meals or leftovers.
  2. Look in the fridge: plan the first meals around what needs using up.
  3. Pick themes: for example pasta, soup, a vegetarian dish and a “fridge clean-out” night.
  4. Cook once, eat twice: double a recipe and plan the second portion for lunch.
  5. Write the grocery list straight from the plan, sorted by category.

Grocery list by category

The list is divided into produce, protein, dairy, pantry and other – roughly the way most supermarkets are laid out. Writing items in the right section saves walking back and forth in the store.

Choose your meals

Select breakfast, lunch and dinner, add a row for snacks, or plan only lunch and dinner – or only dinner if that is the meal you need help with. The rows resize automatically so every cell has room to write.

Stick it on the fridge

A printed plan on the fridge answers the daily “What's for dinner?” question for the whole household. Landscape format works best there; portrait suits planners and binders.

Questions

Can I start the week on Sunday?

Yes – switch “Week starts on” to Sunday. Enter a start date to have the dates printed above each day.

Is there a version without the grocery list?

Untick “Grocery list” and the meal grid uses the full page.
