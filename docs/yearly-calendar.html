Yearly calendar

The whole year on a single sheet – perfect for planning holidays, deadlines and long-term goals.

Why a year-at-a-glance page?

Monthly and weekly planners are great for the details, but they hide the big picture. A one-page yearly calendar shows how long until a deadline, which weeks are already busy and where a holiday would fit best. Pin it next to your desk or put it at the front of your planner.

Ways to use it

  • Holiday planning: mark school breaks and public holidays, then look for long weekends.
  • Goal tracking: colour in each day you exercise, practise or write – the year fills up as a visible streak.
  • Year in pixels: give each day a colour for your mood or energy and see patterns over months.
  • Countdowns: highlight a wedding, move or exam date and cross off the days.

Portrait or landscape?

Portrait arranges the months in four rows of three, which suits binders and planners. Landscape uses three rows of four and leaves larger numbers – better for walls and noticeboards.

Leap years

February gets its 29th day automatically in leap years. A year is a leap year when it is divisible by 4 – except for years divisible by 100, unless they are also divisible by 400. So 2028 is a leap year, while 2100 is not.

For day-by-day planning, create a matching monthly calendar in the same accent colour.

Questions

Can I print a calendar for a past year?

Yes – enter any year between 1900 and 2100, for example to document a past year in a scrapbook.

Are weekends highlighted?

Weekend dates appear in your accent colour and can be shaded; switch the shading off in the settings if you prefer a cleaner look.
