Yearly calendar

The whole year on a single sheet – perfect for planning vacations, deadlines and long-term goals.

Why a year-at-a-glance page?

Monthly and weekly planners are great for the details, but they hide the big picture. A one-page yearly calendar shows how many weeks are left until a deadline, which months are already crowded and where a vacation would fit best. It is also the quickest way to count: the number of school days in a term, the Fridays until a move, the weeks between two appointments. Pin it next to your desk or put it at the front of a binder. From October on, the generator suggests the coming year, so you can plan ahead.

How to set it up

  1. Enter the year. Any year from 1900 to 2100 works.
  2. Choose Monday or Sunday as the first day of each week row.
  3. Pick portrait or landscape. Portrait arranges the months in four rows of three, which suits binders; landscape uses three rows of four and fits a wall or noticeboard.
  4. Keep weekend shading on to see weekends as stripes through the year, or switch it off for a cleaner look and more room for colored markings.
  5. Choose an accent color for month names and weekend dates.

A filled example: planning vacation days

Someone with 15 vacation days for 2027 prints the year in landscape. First, the public holidays from the employer's list are circled – in the US, our 2027 calendar guide shows that Independence Day is observed on Monday, July 5, and Christmas on Friday, December 24. Next to those, bridge days become obvious: taking Tuesday to Friday of the same week as Monday, July 5 turns four vacation days into a nine-day break. The remaining days are shaded in two other blocks, and a running total is written in the corner: 4 + 5 + 6 = 15. The page stays at the front of the planner all year.

Other ways to use it

  • Year in pixels: give each day a color for mood, energy or sleep and look for patterns across months.
  • Streak calendar: fill in each day you exercised, practiced an instrument or wrote – the year fills up visibly.
  • Countdowns: highlight a wedding, exam or moving date and cross off the days.
  • Shared custody or shift schedule: two colors show at a glance who is where.
  • School year: shade term dates and breaks, then count the teaching weeks left before each exam.

Leap years

February gets its 29th day automatically in leap years. A year is a leap year when it is divisible by 4, except for years divisible by 100 unless they are also divisible by 400. So 2027 has 365 days, 2028 is a leap year, and 2100 is not. If you print a calendar for 2028, February 29 falls on a Tuesday.

How small are the days?

Twelve months on one sheet leave little room per day. The generator sets the day numbers at up to 8 pt and shrinks them when the cells get smaller. The table shows the size of one day cell:

PageDay cellDay numbers
A4 portrait / landscape8.3 × 7.8 mm / 9.1 × 6.5 mm8 pt
US Letter portrait / landscape8.6 × 7.1 mm / 8.5 × 6.8 mm8 pt
A5 portrait / landscape5.6 × 4.4 mm / 6.2 × 3.4 mm7.1 pt / 5.4 pt
Half Letter portrait / landscape5.2 × 4.7 mm / 6.4 × 2.9 mm7.5 pt / 4.7 pt

On A5 and Half Letter, portrait is clearly the better choice: in landscape the numbers drop below 6 pt, which many people find hard to read. On A4 and Letter, a cell of about 7–8 mm takes a colored dot, a short stroke of a highlighter or a small round sticker of about 6 mm.

Making a large wall planner

Because the PDF is a vector file, it can be enlarged without blurring. A copy shop can print an A4 page on A3 at 141%, or a US Letter page on Tabloid (11 × 17 in) at about 129%; everything grows in proportion, and A3 cells become about 12 × 11 mm in portrait. Ask for the PDF to be printed at that exact scale rather than “fit to page”, so the margins stay even. On a home printer, the same result needs a poster or tiling option, which glues four sheets together – fiddly, but it works.

Paper and pens

Highlighters can smear fresh inkjet prints, so let the page dry for a few minutes or test on a corner first. For a planner you mark with dots and stickers all year, 160–200 g/m² card stock stays flat on a wall and survives repeated sticker removal. Fine-liners of 0.3 mm or less fit a short note into a cell; for anything longer, write the details on a monthly calendar and only a dot on the year page.

Printing tips

Day numbers on a one-page year are small, so this is the page where a sharp printer setting pays off: print at 100% rather than letting the printer shrink the page, and use the dark-gray default text. For a wall version, 120–176 g/m² paper keeps the sheet flat. For day-by-day planning, create a matching monthly calendar in the same accent color.

Questions

Can I print a calendar for a past year?

Yes – enter any year between 1900 and 2100, for example to document a past year in a scrapbook.

Are weekends highlighted?

Weekend dates appear in your accent color and can be shaded; switch the shading off in the settings if you prefer a cleaner look.

Does the yearly calendar show week numbers?

No, there is no room for them on one page. Use the monthly calendar if you need ISO week numbers.

Is there space to write on it?

Only small marks and colors fit into the day cells. For appointments with text, combine it with monthly or weekly pages.

How do I print it as a large wall planner?

Print the PDF on A3 (from A4) at 141% or on Tabloid (from US Letter) at about 129%, at a copy shop or on a large-format printer. The vector PDF stays sharp at any size.

Which paper size is still easy to read?

A4 and US Letter in either orientation, with 8 pt day numbers. On A5 and Half Letter, choose portrait; landscape makes the numbers very small.

Does the yearly calendar show holidays?

No. Mark them yourself with a colored dot; the US federal holidays for 2027 are listed in our 2027 calendar guide.
