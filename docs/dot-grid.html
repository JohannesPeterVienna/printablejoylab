Dot grid paper

Structure without clutter: dots guide your lines, layouts and lettering, then step back so your writing stands out.

Why dot grid?

Dot grid paper sits between lined and blank. The dots give you a grid for straight lines, boxes and tables, but they are quiet enough that drawings and handwriting stay in the foreground. That flexibility made dot grid the standard paper for bullet journaling.

Choosing the spacing

  • 5 mm is the bullet journal standard used by most dotted notebooks. Two dots are roughly one line of handwriting.
  • 4 mm suits small handwriting and dense layouts like habit grids.
  • 7 mm or 1 cm works well for lettering practice, children and large headings.
  • 1/4 inch matches US dotted notebooks and planner inserts.

Fine, medium or bold dots?

If your printer prints lightly or you plan to photocopy the pages, choose medium or bold dots so they don't vanish. For fountain pens and watercolour lettering, fine dots in a light colour are almost invisible once the page is filled.

Bullet journal layouts to try

  • Future log: divide a page into six boxes for the next six months.
  • Weekly spread: seven boxes of equal height plus a to-do column.
  • Tracker grid: one dot per day across, one row per habit.
  • Collections: books to read, gift ideas, packing lists.

Making a dotted notebook

Print a stack of pages, fold A4 sheets in half for an A5 booklet, or punch them for a ring binder. Thick 100–120 g/m² paper reduces ghosting when you use markers.

Questions

What size dots do bullet journals use?

Most dotted notebooks use a 5 mm grid. That is our default setting.

Will the dots show when I scan my notes?

Light, fine dots usually disappear or turn very faint in scans – one reason dot grid is popular for notes you want to digitise.
