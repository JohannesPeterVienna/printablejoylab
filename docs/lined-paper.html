Lined paper

Choose the ruling, paper size and line colour, then download a crisp vector PDF or print straight away.

Which ruling should you pick?

Lined paper is described by the distance between its lines. The three standard rulings come from US school paper, but they are used everywhere:

  • Wide ruled – 11/32 inch (8.7 mm). Generous space for larger handwriting, younger writers and anyone who finds tight lines tiring.
  • College ruled – 9/32 inch (7.1 mm). The most common choice for notes, letters and journals. It fits more text without feeling cramped.
  • Narrow ruled – 1/4 inch (6.35 mm). Popular with people who write small or want to squeeze a lot onto one page.

Need something else? Pick Custom and enter any spacing between 3 and 20 mm. Many European school exercise books use around 8–9 mm, while 6 mm suits compact note-taking.

Margin line and header

The red margin line gives you a column for dates, question numbers or notes in the margin – it is the classic look of exercise paper. The optional header adds a Name and Date line at the top, which is handy for worksheets, exam practice and letters.

Light lines write better

Very dark lines compete with your handwriting. For everyday writing, the light grey setting is usually the most pleasant; choose Blue for the familiar notebook look or Medium if your printer prints faintly. When you scan or photocopy your notes later, light lines also disappear more cleanly.

Printing tips

Select the same paper size here as in your printer, and set scaling to 100 % / Actual size rather than “Fit to page”. That keeps the spacing exactly as chosen. Our PDFs are vector files, so lines stay razor-sharp on any printer and at any zoom level.

Printing double-sided for a notebook? Print odd pages first, flip the stack and print again – or just use the same page on both sides, since lined paper has no front or back.

Questions

Is college ruled the same as 7 mm lines?

Almost: college ruling is 9/32 inch, which is 7.14 mm. For most purposes the difference to 7 mm is invisible.

Can I use the paper commercially?
