Daily planner

Time-block your day: an hourly schedule on the left, priorities, to-dos and notes on the right.

Time blocking in a nutshell

A to-do list tells you what to do; a schedule tells you when. Time blocking combines both: you give your most important tasks a slot in the day, just like a meeting. It protects focused work, and it makes it obvious when a day is already full – if the schedule has no gap left, a new task has to replace something rather than squeeze in. This page puts the schedule on the left and everything else on the right, so you see both at once.

What is on the page

  • Schedule: one line per hour, or per half hour with dashed lines in between. On A4 with the default 6:00 to 21:00, each hour gets about 15 mm.
  • Top 3 priorities: three numbered lines for the tasks that make the day a success.
  • To do: a list with check boxes – 11 on A4 – for small tasks.
  • Notes, “Grateful for” and a water tracker with eight glasses; both extras can be switched off.

How to fill in the page

  1. Write the three priorities first. If you only do these, the day was a success.
  2. Block appointments in the schedule, then give each priority its own block, ideally at the time of day when you concentrate best.
  3. Batch small tasks: collect emails, calls and errands in the to-do list and give them one or two blocks.
  4. Capture loose ends in Notes so they do not distract you.
  5. In the evening, tick what is done and move the rest deliberately to tomorrow.

A filled example: a working day with a deadline

The page is dated for a Wednesday with a report due. Priorities: 1. finish the report draft, 2. send it to the team, 3. book the venue for next month. The schedule shows a 9:00 stand-up (30 minutes), the draft blocked from 9:30 to 12:00, lunch, a client call at 14:00, and “email + calls” from 15:00 to 15:45 for the to-do items. At 16:00 the report is sent, at 16:15 the venue is booked. The notes box holds a phone number from the call; the gratitude box says “quiet morning to write”.

Adjust the schedule to your day

Early riser? Start the schedule as early as 4:00. Night owl? Run it until midnight. The planner divides the available height evenly, so a shorter day gets more room per hour: 8:00 to 18:00 on A4 gives about 22 mm per hour, enough for two lines of text in each slot. Half-hour slots suit days full of short appointments, such as a clinic shift or a day of back-to-back calls.

Printing tips

The page is always portrait. A5 or Half Letter turns it into a pocket-sized day card, though the hours get tighter, so shorten the schedule to the hours you really need. Print a small stack of undated pages for normal days and a dated one for special days such as a move, a trip or an exam. Plan the week first with the weekly planner, then use this page only on days that need hour-by-hour detail.

Questions

Can I make the schedule start earlier?

Yes – choose any start hour from 4:00 and any end hour up to 24:00.

Is there a version without the water tracker?

Untick “Water tracker” and “Gratitude & reflection” and the notes area grows automatically.

Should I fill in every hour?

No. Block appointments and priorities, and leave the rest open. A schedule packed from start to finish usually breaks by lunchtime.

Does the water tracker set a target?

It has eight glasses to tick as a simple visual reminder. It is not a medical recommendation; how much you need depends on you.
