Daily planner

Time-block your day: an hourly schedule on the left, priorities, to-dos and notes on the right.

Time blocking in a nutshell

A to-do list tells you what to do; a schedule tells you when. Time blocking combines both: you give your most important tasks a slot in the day, just like a meeting. It protects focused work and makes it obvious when a day is already full.

How to fill in the page

  1. Top 3 priorities: write down the three things that matter most today. If you only do these, the day was a success.
  2. Schedule: block time for appointments first, then for your priorities. Choose half-hour slots for busy days.
  3. To do: collect the smaller tasks – emails, calls, errands – and batch them into one or two blocks.
  4. Notes: capture ideas and loose ends so they do not distract you.
  5. Evening: tick off what is done and note one thing you are grateful for.

Adjust the schedule to your day

Early riser? Start the schedule at 5:00. Night owl? Run it until midnight. The planner divides the available space evenly, so a shorter day gets more room per hour.

The small extras

The water tracker has eight glasses to tick off – a simple visual nudge, not a medical recommendation. The gratitude box takes a minute at the end of the day; many people find it a pleasant way to close the page. Switch both off if you need more space for notes.

Dated or undated

Enter a date to print a page for a specific day – for example an important event or a travel day. Leave it empty to print a stack of undated pages for everyday use.

Questions

Can I make the schedule start earlier?

Yes – choose any start hour from 4:00 and any end hour up to 24:00.
