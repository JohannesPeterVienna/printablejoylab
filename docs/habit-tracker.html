Habit tracker

Type your habits, pick the month and fill in a circle every day you keep your promise to yourself.

Why tracking works

A habit tracker makes progress visible. Every filled circle is a small reward, and an unbroken row – a streak – becomes something you do not want to break. The act of ticking also forces a daily moment of reflection: did I do it today?

How long does a habit take?

The popular “21 days” rule is a myth. In a well-known study by Phillippa Lally and colleagues at University College London (published 2010), participants needed a median of 66 days before a new behaviour felt automatic – with a wide range from 18 to 254 days. Missing a single day did not significantly harm the process. So track for at least two or three months, and do not give up after a slip.

Tips for a tracker that sticks

  • Start small: three to five habits are plenty. Too many rows turn the tracker into a guilt list.
  • Make habits concrete: “Read 20 pages” works better than “Read more”.
  • Keep the tracker visible: on the fridge, next to your toothbrush or inside your planner.
  • Never miss twice: one missed day is normal; two in a row is the start of a new pattern.
  • Review monthly: use the “Done” column to count your days and decide what to keep, change or drop.

Questions

How many habits can I track?

Up to 15 named habits plus up to 10 empty rows – but most people do best with three to seven.

Can I track habits weekly instead of daily?

Use the checklist template with numbered lines for weekly goals, or mark only certain days on the monthly tracker.
