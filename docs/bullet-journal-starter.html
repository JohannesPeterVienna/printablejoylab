Bullet journal for beginners: a starter kit you can print

The core ideas of bullet journaling and the pages to begin with.

Bullet journaling is a flexible paper planning method created by designer Ryder Carroll. At its heart it is simple: short notes, a few symbols and pages you design yourself. You do not need artistic skills or expensive notebooks – a stack of dot grid paper is enough.

Rapid logging

Instead of long sentences, you write short bullets marked with symbols:

  • • a task – later marked with × when done or > when moved
  • ○ an event or appointment
  • – a note or idea

That is all you need to start. Many people add their own symbols over time.

The core pages

  1. Index: the first pages list where things are, so you can find them later.
  2. Future log: events and goals for the coming months. A yearly calendar works perfectly here.
  3. Monthly log: a calendar page and a task list for the month – try our monthly calendar.
  4. Weekly or daily log: where the actual planning happens. Start with a weekly planner page.
  5. Collections: lists that matter to you – books, ideas, packing lists. Use the checklist maker.

Trackers

Habit, mood or sleep trackers are popular additions. Keep them few and simple at first; a habit tracker for three or four habits is a good start.

Printable or notebook?

A bound dotted notebook is the classic choice. Printed pages in a ring binder are more forgiving: you can rearrange, replace and add pages, and you can try different layouts without “ruining” a notebook. Print 5 mm dot grid paper for your own designs and the ready-made planners for everything else.

Keep it sustainable

Beautiful spreads on social media can be intimidating. The method works with plain handwriting and a ruler. Spend your time planning, not decorating – unless decorating is what you enjoy.
