Guides

Practical know-how for printing, planning and paper.

These four guides answer the questions that come up most often once you start printing your own planners and paper. Each one takes only a few minutes to read and links to the templates it talks about. Here is what each guide covers, so you can go straight to the one you need.

Which guide should you read?

  • Your grid prints too small, or the bottom of your calendar is cut off? Start with How to print at actual size. It explains why A4 and US Letter are different shapes, which scale setting to choose on Windows, on a Mac, in Adobe Acrobat Reader and on phones and tablets, how to check the result with a ruler, and which paper weight suits planners you write on with markers.
  • Not sure which paper pattern to print? Lined, grid, dot or isometric compares the four patterns and their common spacings – from college ruling to 5 mm dot grid – and ends with quick recommendations for lecture notes, math homework, handwriting practice, journaling, floor plans and product sketches.
  • Curious about bullet journaling? The bullet journal starter kit covers rapid logging with its three basic symbols, the core pages (index, future log, monthly and weekly log, collections), simple trackers, and the pros and cons of printed pages in a ring binder compared with a bound notebook.
  • Wondering whether planning on paper still makes sense? Paper or app? looks at what paper does well, what studies on handwriting suggest, where apps are the better tool, and a simple routine that combines a digital calendar with a printed weekly page.

If you are new to printables, read the print guide first: the right scale setting makes the biggest difference to every template on this site.
