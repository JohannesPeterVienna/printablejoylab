Graph paper

Square grid paper for math, sketching, floor plans and pixel art – in metric or imperial sizes.

Metric or imperial?

Graph paper (also called grid or squared paper) comes in two families. Metric grids of 4 mm, 5 mm or 1 cm are standard in most of Europe and in the sciences. Imperial grids divide each inch into 4 or 5 squares: 1/4 inch (6.35 mm) is the classic US quad-ruled paper used in schools, and 1/5 inch (5.08 mm) is common on engineering pads. Choose Custom for anything from 2 to 25 mm. The grid is centered on the page, so the white border is the same on both sides. With 5 mm squares, a portrait A4 page holds 37 × 53 squares and a US Letter page 38 × 50.

Bold lines make counting easy

A heavier line every few squares turns the grid into a ruler. With 5 mm squares and bold lines every 2, each bold square is exactly 1 cm; with bold lines every 10, each bold square is 5 cm. On a 1/4 inch grid, bold lines every 4 squares mark whole inches. Choose No bold lines for pixel art or stitch charts, where every square counts the same, or bold lines every 10 for charts where you count stitches in tens.

How to use it for a scale drawing

  1. Measure the longest side of the room, garden bed or object.
  2. Count how many squares fit along the matching side of the page (landscape usually gives more).
  3. Choose a round scale that fits, such as 1 square = 10 cm or 1 square = 6 inches, and write it in a corner.
  4. Draw outer walls first, then doors and windows, then furniture or plants.

A filled example: a raised garden bed

A gardener plans a 1.2 m × 2.4 m bed on 5 mm graph paper in landscape, with bold lines every 10 squares. At 1 square = 5 cm, the bed is 24 × 48 squares – it fits with room for notes. Each bold square now covers 50 × 50 cm. Tomatoes go along the back in four 50 cm sections, lettuce and radishes in a strip of 6 squares at the front, and a path of 2 squares is marked between them. The planting dates are written beside each section, and the page goes into a plastic sleeve in the shed.

More uses

  • Math and statistics: plotting functions, bar charts and aligned columns of numbers.
  • Knitting, cross-stitch and pixel art: one square per stitch or pixel; a light line color keeps the chart readable after you fill it in.
  • Handwriting: a 1 cm grid helps children keep letters the same size.

Printing tips

Scale matters more for graph paper than for any other template: if the printer shrinks the page to fit its margins, a 5 mm grid becomes about 4.8 mm and scale drawings drift. Print at 100% and check ten squares with a ruler. For engineering-style sheets, pick the light line color; for photocopying, medium or dark.

Questions

How many squares fit on an A4 page?

With a 5 mm grid and our 12 mm margins, the page holds 37 squares across and 53 down in portrait orientation.

What is engineering paper?

Engineering paper is usually a light green 1/5 inch grid printed on the back so it shows through faintly. Choose the 1/5 inch grid and the light line color for a similar look.

Which grid is best for algebra and geometry homework?

A 5 mm or 1/4 inch grid. It is fine enough to fit a whole graph on half a page and large enough to write numbers inside the squares.

Can I get a grid with an exact number of squares?

Choose a custom grid size in half-millimeter steps. The preview redraws instantly, so you can count the squares along an edge before you print.
