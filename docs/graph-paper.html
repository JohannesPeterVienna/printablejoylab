Graph paper

Square grid paper for maths, sketching, floor plans and pixel art – in metric or imperial sizes.

Metric or imperial?

Graph paper (also called grid or squared paper) comes in two families. Metric grids of 5 mm or 1 cm are standard in most of Europe and in science. Imperial grids divide each inch into 4 or 5 squares – “4 squares per inch” (1/4 inch) is the classic US quad-ruled paper used in schools and engineering notebooks.

Bold lines make counting easy

A bold line every 5 or 10 squares turns the grid into a ruler. With 5 mm squares and bold lines every 2, each bold square is exactly one centimetre; with bold lines every 10, each bold square is 5 cm – handy for scale drawings. For quad-ruled imperial paper, bold lines every 4 squares mark whole inches.

What graph paper is good for

  • Maths and statistics: plotting functions, drawing bar charts, keeping columns of numbers aligned.
  • Floor plans and garden layouts: let one square stand for 10 cm or 1 foot and you can sketch rooms to scale.
  • Knitting, cross-stitch and pixel art: each square is one stitch or pixel.
  • Handwriting practice: squares help keep letters evenly sized, which is why many schools use squared exercise books.

Scale drawings in three steps

  1. Measure the longest side of the room or object.
  2. Divide the number of squares that fit across the page by that length – this gives you squares per metre (or foot).
  3. Round down to a convenient scale, such as 1 square = 25 cm, and write it in a corner of the page.

Always print at 100 % scale. If the printer shrinks the page to fit its margins, a “5 mm” grid becomes 4.8 mm and your scale drawing will be slightly off.

Questions

How many squares fit on an A4 page?

With a 5 mm grid and our 12 mm margins, the page holds 37 squares across and 53 down in portrait orientation.

What is engineering paper?

Engineering paper is usually a light green 1/5 inch grid printed on the back so it shows through faintly. Choose the 1/5 inch grid and the light line colour for a similar look.
