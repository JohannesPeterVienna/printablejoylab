Isometric paper

A grid of 60° lines that makes three-dimensional sketches easy – for design, architecture, math and tabletop games.

What is isometric paper?

Isometric paper is covered in equilateral triangles. In the line version, three sets of lines run vertically and at 30° to either side of horizontal. Because all three axes are drawn at the same scale, cubes and boxes look three-dimensional without vanishing points, and every edge can be measured by counting triangles. The triangle size you choose is the length of one side: a 5 mm triangle is about 4.33 mm high, a 1 cm triangle about 8.66 mm.

How to draw a cube

  1. Pick a point where lines meet. Draw a vertical edge along a vertical line, for example three triangles long.
  2. From the top and bottom of that edge, follow the two diagonal lines – one to the left, one to the right – for the same number of triangles.
  3. Close the sides with two more vertical edges and copy the top diagonals to finish the lid.

Every edge follows the grid, so the proportions are always right. Larger shapes are stacks of cubes: draw the overall block first, then cut away the parts you do not need. Shade the three visible faces in three tones – light on top, medium on one side, dark on the other – and the object immediately looks solid.

A filled example: planning a bookshelf

A maker plans a shelf 90 cm wide, 30 cm deep and 200 cm tall on 5 mm isometric paper. At 1 triangle side = 10 cm, the shelf is 9 triangles along one diagonal axis, 3 along the other and 20 up the vertical lines. The outer box is drawn first, then four shelves at 40 cm intervals, each 4 triangles above the last, with the real measurements written next to them. Hidden edges are dashed. The finished sketch shows at a glance whether the depth looks right before any wood is cut.

Who uses it?

  • Designers and architects for product sketches and room layouts.
  • Game designers and tabletop players for isometric maps, dungeons and pixel-art style scenes.
  • Teachers for volume, nets of solids and spatial reasoning tasks.
  • Makers planning woodwork, 3D prints or LEGO builds.

Lines or dots?

Full lines are easiest for beginners. The dot version shows only the points where lines would cross: drawings look cleaner and the grid disappears into the background, which suits finished illustrations. In the dot version the rows run horizontally, offset by half a triangle, so turn the sheet a quarter turn if you want vertical edges to follow a column of dots.

How the grid is measured

On the line version, the vertical lines are spaced at the height of one triangle, not its side: with 5 mm triangles they sit about 4.33 mm apart, with 1 cm triangles about 8.66 mm. Along any of the three line directions, however, the crossings are exactly one triangle side apart. So you measure an edge by counting steps along the line it follows. A portrait page holds 43 vertical grid lines on A4 and 45 on US Letter with 5 mm triangles; with 1 cm triangles 22 and 23.

Three views and one isometric drawing

Technical drawing classes often pair isometric paper with ordinary graph paper. The front, top and side views of an object go on graph paper, one square per unit; the isometric view goes on this sheet, one triangle side per unit. Building the isometric view from the three flat views is a classic spatial-reasoning exercise: every corner in the flat views becomes a crossing point on the triangle grid. Using the same unit on both sheets lets you check each edge by counting.

Isometric games and pixel art

Many video games described as “isometric” actually use a 2:1 pixel slope – two pixels across for one up, an angle of about 26.6° – because it gives clean pixel steps. Our grid is true isometric, with lines at 30°. For true isometric sketches, maps and board-game tiles it is exactly right; for planning 2:1 pixel art, use graph paper and step two squares across for every square up.

Paper for shading and tracing

For shading the three faces of an object with colored pencils, a smooth 100–120 g/m² paper takes several layers without pilling. The dot version uses dots of about 0.56 mm, larger than the fine dots of our dot grid, so they stay visible after shading. If you use the sheet as a drawing underlay on a light pad, print the lines in the dark color.

Choosing the triangle size and printing

4–5 mm triangles give lots of detail for small objects; 7 mm or 1 cm triangles are easier to see and suit classroom use, children and large maps. Print at 100% so the 60° angles and the scale stay exact – a page shrunk to fit a different paper size distorts nothing visibly but makes measured drawings slightly off. A light line color keeps your pencil lines readable; use medium if you plan to photocopy the sheet.

Questions

Is isometric the same as perspective drawing?

No. In perspective drawing, parallel lines meet in a vanishing point. In isometric drawing they stay parallel, so measurements along each axis remain to scale.

Can I use the grid for hex maps?

Yes – groups of six triangles form hexagons, so isometric paper works for simple hex maps too.

Which triangle size should children use?

1 cm triangles. They are large enough to count easily and to color in, and simple cube buildings stay clearly readable.

Why do circles look like ovals on isometric drawings?

A circle on the face of a cube is seen at an angle, so in isometric view it becomes an ellipse. Sketch it inside a rhombus of grid lines and round the corners.

How do I measure a slanted edge?

Count the triangle sides along the line the edge follows. With a 5 mm grid, an edge of six steps along a 30° line is 30 mm in the drawing, just like six steps up a vertical line.

Is this the same grid video games use?

Not quite. Many games use a 2:1 pixel slope of about 26.6°. This paper is true isometric with 30° lines, which is what technical drawing and most board-game maps use.

Why are the vertical lines closer together than the triangle size?

Their spacing is the triangle height, which is the side × √3 ÷ 2 – about 4.33 mm for a 5 mm triangle. Measurements along the lines still use the full triangle side.
