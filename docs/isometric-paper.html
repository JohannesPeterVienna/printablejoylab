Isometric paper

A grid of 60° lines that makes three-dimensional sketches easy – for design, architecture, maths and tabletop games.

What is isometric paper?

Isometric paper is covered in equilateral triangles. Three sets of lines run vertically and at 30° to either side of horizontal. Because all three axes are drawn at the same scale, cubes and boxes look three-dimensional without any vanishing points – perfect for quick technical sketches.

How to draw a cube

  1. Pick a point where lines meet. Draw a vertical edge along a vertical line.
  2. From the top and bottom of that edge, follow the two diagonal lines – one to the left, one to the right – for the same number of triangles.
  3. Close the shape with vertical edges and copy the top diagonals to finish the lid.

Every edge follows the grid, so the proportions are always right. Bigger shapes are just stacks of cubes.

Who uses it?

  • Designers and architects for product sketches and room layouts.
  • Game designers for isometric maps and pixel art.
  • Teachers for volume, nets and spatial reasoning tasks.
  • Makers planning woodwork or 3D prints.

Lines or dots?

Full lines are easiest for beginners. The dot version shows only the points where lines would cross: your drawings look cleaner and the grid disappears into the background – a good choice for finished illustrations.

Choosing the triangle size

5 mm triangles give lots of detail for small objects. 7 mm or 1 cm triangles are easier to see and suit classroom use or large maps. Print at 100 % scale so the angles stay exact.

Questions

Is isometric the same as perspective drawing?

No. In perspective drawing, parallel lines meet in a vanishing point. In isometric drawing they stay parallel, so measurements along each axis remain to scale.

Can I use the grid for hex maps?

Yes – groups of six triangles form hexagons, so isometric paper works for simple hex maps too.
