Budget planner

Write down what you plan to spend, track what you actually spent and see the difference at a glance – with your own categories.

How the budget page works

Each row has three columns: Planned (what you expect or allow), Actual (what really happened) and Difference. At the end of the month, the differences show where your money went differently than planned – the starting point for next month's budget.

Setting up your categories

Edit the category list to match your life. Start with fixed costs such as rent, utilities, insurance and subscriptions; then add variable spending such as groceries, transport, eating out and shopping. Include savings as a category of its own – paying yourself first is easier when it is a line on the page.

A popular rule of thumb: 50/30/20

One widely used guideline splits take-home pay into about 50 % for needs (housing, food, bills), 30 % for wants and 20 % for savings and paying off debt. It is a starting point, not a rule – in expensive cities housing alone can take half of the income. The point is to choose your split consciously.

Tips for sticking with it

  • Track weekly: ten minutes every Sunday with your bank app is easier than reconstructing a whole month.
  • Round up: exact cents are not important; trends are.
  • Plan irregular costs: divide annual bills such as insurance or gifts by twelve and budget that amount every month.
  • Review, don't judge: an overspent category is information for next month, not a failure.

The planner is an organising tool and not financial advice.
