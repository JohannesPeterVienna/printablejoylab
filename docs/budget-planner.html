Budget planner

Write down what you plan to spend, track what you actually spent and see the difference at a glance – with your own categories.

How the budget page works

Each row has three columns: Planned (what you expect or allow), Actual (what really happened) and Difference. At the end of the month, the differences show where your money went differently than planned – the starting point for next month's budget. Above the tables, four summary boxes hold the month's totals: Income, Expenses, Saved and Left over. The income table has rows for salary, side income, other income and a total; the expense table lists your own categories, followed by a total line and a box for notes and goals.

How to set it up

  1. Choose the month, or All 12 months for a 12-page budget binder with your categories on every page.
  2. Pick the currency symbol for the summary boxes: $, €, £ or none.
  3. Edit the expense categories, one per line – up to 18. The default list starts with rent or mortgage, utilities and groceries and ends with savings and other.
  4. Change the title if you like, for example to a household name.

With 13 categories on A4, each expense row is about 10 mm high; with 18 it shrinks to about 8 mm, still enough for handwritten amounts.

Setting up your categories

Start with fixed costs – rent, utilities, insurance, phone, subscriptions – and then variable spending such as groceries, transport, eating out and shopping. Keep savings as a category of its own, so paying yourself first is a visible line. Irregular costs like car repairs or gifts are easier when you divide the yearly amount by twelve and plan that amount every month.

A filled example: one month

A household plans $4,200 of income and fills in: rent $1,450 planned and actual; groceries $600 planned, $655 actual (difference −$55); eating out $150 planned, $90 actual (+$60); transport $220 planned, $240 actual (−$20); savings $500 planned and actual. At the end of the month the Expenses box reads $3,980 against $4,000 planned, and Left over shows $220. The note under “Notes & goals” says: “Groceries over twice in a row – raise to $640, lower eating out to $110.” The example is made up to show the method; your categories and numbers will be different.

A popular rule of thumb: 50/30/20

One widely used guideline splits take-home pay into about 50% for needs, 30% for wants and 20% for savings and paying off debt. It is a starting point, not a rule: in expensive cities housing alone can take half of the income. The point is to choose your split consciously and write it into the Planned column.

Tips for sticking with it

  • Update weekly: ten minutes with your bank statement on a fixed day is easier than reconstructing a whole month.
  • Round to whole amounts: trends matter more than cents.
  • Review, don't judge: an overspent category is information for next month.

Printing tips

The page is always portrait and works on A4, US Letter, A5 or Half Letter; the larger sizes leave more room for amounts. Print a year at once and file each month behind the last, so you can compare the same category across months.

The planner is an organizing tool and not financial advice.

Questions

Can I use a different currency?

Choose $, €, £ or no symbol. The summary boxes show the symbol; amounts in the table are written by hand.

Can I print a whole year?

Choose “All 12 months” for a 12-page budget binder with your categories on every page.

Can I rename the income rows?

The income rows are fixed (salary, side income, other, total), but you can cross one out and write your own label; the expense categories are fully editable.

What goes into “Saved” and “Left over”?

Saved is what you moved to savings this month (it also appears as a category). Left over is total income minus total expenses, savings included – money still available or carried into next month.
