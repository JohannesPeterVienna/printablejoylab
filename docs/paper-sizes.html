Paper sizes and print scale

Look up a sheet size, or calculate what a print setting does to your page.

Print scale calculator

Pick the page size stored in your PDF, the paper that is in the printer and the setting in the print dialog. The calculator shows the scale the page is printed at, what a grid or line spacing turns into, and whether anything is cut off.

Drawing: gray = sheet, dashed = area the printer can reach, red = the printed page, all to one scale.

How it is calculated. At 100% the page keeps its size; whatever is wider or taller than the area the printer can reach is lost. With a “Fit” setting the program divides the reachable width by the page width and the reachable height by the page height and uses the smaller of the two numbers as the scale. “Shrink oversized pages” does the same but never goes above 100%. The reachable area is the sheet minus the printer's blank border on each side.

What it assumes. Page and sheet have the same orientation, the page is centered, and the border is the same on all four sides. Real printers often have a wider border on one side. You can measure yours with the edge rulers on the print test page and enter the largest value.

How to read the columns

  • Millimeters and inches. One of the two is the defining value: the Printer Working Group list gives ISO and Japanese sizes in millimeters and North American sizes in inches. The other column is converted with 1 inch = 25.4 mm, which is exact by definition, and rounded to one decimal (mm) or two decimals (inches).
  • PDF points. A PDF describes its page in units of 1/72 inch unless the file says otherwise. A4 is therefore 595.3 × 841.9 points and US Letter 612 × 792 points – the numbers you meet in PDF tools and print drivers.
  • Sides. Long side divided by short side. Two sizes with the same value have the same shape, and one can be scaled onto the other without blank strips.
  • PWG name. The self-describing media name from PWG 5101.1. It contains the size itself, for example iso_a4_210x297mm.
  • CSS keyword. Ten sizes have a keyword for the CSS size property, as in @page { size: A4 }. All other sizes have to be written as lengths.
  • Our generators. The four sizes in which every template on this site can be made. The link opens graph paper in that size.

What the numbers show

These statements are recalculated from the table every time the site is built; if one stopped being true, the build would fail.

  • Halving. In the A, B, C, JIS B, RA and SRA rows, each size is the next larger one cut in half across its long side, with half millimeters rounded down. A4 (210 × 297) halves to A5 (148 × 210), because 297 ÷ 2 = 148.5.
  • A0 is one square meter, almost. 841 × 1189 mm is 0.999949 m². B0 is exactly 1000 mm wide.
  • The shape stays the same. From A0 to A6 and B0 to B6 the sides stay within 0.006 of 1 : 1.414 (the square root of 2). That is why an A4 page reduced to A5 fills the sheet. US sizes do not behave this way: US Letter is 1 : 1.294, Half Letter 1 : 1.545, US Legal 1 : 1.647 and Ledger 1 : 1.545. A Letter page reduced onto Half Letter leaves blank strips; try it in the calculator above.
  • C sits between A and B. Every C size is within 1 mm of the geometric mean of the A and B size with the same number, so an unfolded A4 sheet (210 × 297 mm) fits inside a C4 envelope (229 × 324 mm).
  • A4 and US Letter. US Letter is 5.9 mm wider and 17.6 mm shorter than A4. Fitting an A4 page onto Letter therefore gives 94.1%, and fitting a Letter page onto A4 gives 97.3%.
