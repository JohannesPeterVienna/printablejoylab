Lined, grid, dot or isometric: which paper for which job?

A quick guide to choosing the right printable paper.

Paper with lines, squares or dots is not just a matter of taste. Each pattern makes certain tasks easier. Here is a quick guide.

Lined paper – for writing

Lines guide your handwriting and keep text straight. Choose college ruling (7.1 mm) for everyday notes, wide ruling (8.7 mm) for larger handwriting and narrow ruling (6.35 mm) to fit more on a page. → Lined paper

Graph paper – for numbers and plans

Squares keep columns aligned, make charts easy and let you draw to scale. 5 mm suits maths and notes, 1 cm suits children and big sketches, and 1/4 inch is the classic US quad-ruled paper. → Graph paper

Dot grid – for everything in between

Dots give structure for writing, tables and drawings but stay in the background. That is why bullet journalers love it. 5 mm is the standard. → Dot grid paper

Isometric – for 3D

Triangles at 60° make it easy to draw cubes, rooms and objects in three dimensions without perspective. Great for designers, makers and game maps. → Isometric paper

Quick recommendations

  • Lecture notes: college-ruled lined paper or 5 mm dot grid.
  • Maths homework: 5 mm graph paper.
  • Handwriting practice: wide-ruled lines or 1 cm squares.
  • Journaling and planning: 5 mm dot grid.
  • Floor plans: 5 mm or 1/4 inch graph paper with bold lines.
  • Product sketches: isometric paper.

All papers can be printed on A4, US Letter, A5 or Half Letter – choose the size that matches your printer and binder.
