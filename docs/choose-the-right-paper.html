Lined, grid, dot or isometric: which paper for which job?

Rulings, grid sizes and paper weights compared – with gsm to pound conversions.

Reviewed November 3, 2026 · AdStern

Lines, squares, dots and triangles are not just a matter of taste. Each pattern makes one kind of work easier and another slightly harder. This guide compares the four patterns with real measurements, shows how much fits on a page, explains paper weights in both grams per square meter and US pounds, and walks through one practical example from start to finish.

The four patterns at a glance

All four patterns can be printed on A4, US Letter, A5 or Half Letter. The first question is what you will mostly do on the page: write sentences, line up numbers, mix text with boxes and sketches, or draw objects in three dimensions.

PatternTypical spacingBest forLess suited for
Lined6.35–8.73 mm (1/4–11/32 in)Running text, letters, essays, lecture notesTables, diagrams
Graph5 mm, 1 cm, 1/4 in, 1/5 inMath, charts, floor plans, pixel and stitch patternsLong handwritten text (busy background)
Dot grid5 mm (most common), 4–10 mmJournaling, planning layouts, mixed notes and sketchesPrecise technical drawing
Isometric5–10 mm triangle side3D sketches, product ideas, game mapsWriting

Lined paper: the three rulings in numbers

US school rulings are defined in fractions of an inch. Wide ruling is 11/32 in (8.73 mm), college ruling 9/32 in (7.14 mm) and narrow ruling 1/4 in (6.35 mm). The table shows how many writing lines our generator fits on a portrait page with the name and date header switched on.

RulingSpacingLines on A4Lines on US Letter
Wide8.73 mm (11/32 in)2927
College7.14 mm (9/32 in)3533
Narrow6.35 mm (1/4 in)4037

A practical way to choose: write one sentence in your normal handwriting on scrap paper and measure the height of a capital letter. If capitals are taller than about half the spacing, the lines will feel cramped; go one ruling wider.

Graph paper and dot grid: how many squares or dots fit

Grid paper is named after the side of one square. A 1/4 in grid has four squares per inch (6.35 mm) and is the classic US quad ruling; a 1/5 in grid (5.08 mm) is common on engineering pads. Dot grid uses the same idea but prints only the corners. With our margins, a portrait page holds:

PatternA4 portraitUS Letter portrait
5 mm graph paper37 × 53 squares38 × 50 squares
1/4 in graph paper29 × 42 squares30 × 39 squares
1 cm graph paper18 × 26 squares19 × 25 squares
5 mm dot grid38 × 54 dots39 × 51 dots

On dot grid, the dot size matters as much as the spacing. Our fine, medium and bold dots are about 0.36, 0.52 and 0.72 mm across. Fine dots almost disappear under ink; bold dots survive faint printers and photocopies. On isometric paper, a 5 mm triangle has a height of about 4.33 mm (5 × √3 ÷ 2), so rows are slightly closer together than the triangle side suggests.

Paper weight: gsm and pounds

Outside the US, paper weight is given as grammage in grams per square meter (g/m² or gsm). In the US it is given in pounds: the weight of 500 sheets in the paper grade's “basis size”. Because bond, text and cover paper have different basis sizes, the same pound number means different paper. That is why a 65 lb cover stock is much heavier than 24 lb bond. The conversions below use the standard basis sizes (bond 17 × 22 in, text 25 × 38 in, cover 20 × 26 in) and are rounded.

US labelApprox. gsmTypical use for printables
20 lb bond75 g/m²Everyday copy paper; fine for lists and test prints
24 lb bond (≈ 60 lb text)90 g/m²Planner pages written on both sides with ballpoint or gel pen
28 lb bond (≈ 70 lb text)105 g/m²Less show-through with fineliners and light markers
32 lb bond (≈ 80 lb text)120 g/m²Notebook feel; good for dot grid journals
65 lb cover176 g/m²Covers, dividers, calendars on a wall

The common European office paper of 80 g/m² sits between the first two rows, at roughly 21 lb bond. Before you load heavier paper, check the maximum weight in your printer's specifications; many home printers handle 120 g/m² from the main tray but need the rear or manual feed for card stock.

Worked example: a floor plan on graph paper

You want to sketch a bedroom of 4.2 m × 3.6 m and try out furniture positions.

  1. Pick the grid: 5 mm graph paper, landscape. On A4 landscape the page holds 54 × 36 squares; on US Letter landscape 51 × 37.
  2. Pick the scale: with 1 square = 10 cm, the room needs 42 × 36 squares. That fits on both sizes, with 9–12 squares to spare for labels on the long side.
  3. Use bold lines: set a bold line every 10 squares, so each bold square equals exactly 1 m. Counting becomes much faster.
  4. Draw the furniture to the same scale: a 90 × 200 cm bed is 9 × 20 squares. Cut the pieces out of a second sheet and move them around before you move anything heavy.

Working in feet? Choose the 1/4 in grid and let one square stand for 6 in: a 14 × 12 ft room then needs 28 × 24 squares, which fits on a Letter page in either orientation.

Quick recommendations

  • Lecture notes: college-ruled lines, or 5 mm dot grid if you draw diagrams.
  • Math homework: 5 mm or 1/4 in graph paper.
  • Handwriting practice for children: wide ruling or a 1 cm grid.
  • Journaling and planning: 5 mm dot grid, medium dots.
  • Product and room sketches: isometric paper with 7 mm triangles.

FAQ

Is college ruled the same as 7 mm?

Nearly: 9/32 in is 7.14 mm. Over a whole page the difference adds up to only a few millimeters.

Why is 65 lb cover heavier than 80 lb text paper?

Because the pound figure is the weight of 500 sheets in each grade's own basis size, and those sizes differ: cover is weighed at 20 × 26 in, text at 25 × 38 in and bond at 17 × 22 in. The text sheet is much larger, so the same pounds are spread over more paper. Converted to grams per square meter, 65 lb cover is about 176 g/m², while 80 lb text is only about 118 g/m². When you compare papers of different grades, always look at the gsm value.

Which paper stops markers bleeding through?

No plain office paper fully stops alcohol-based markers. Heavier paper (105–120 g/m²) reduces show-through for fineliners and water-based markers; for alcohol markers, print on one side only.

Sources and standards

  • ISO 216 – A-series paper sizes (A4, A5) and the 1 : √2 aspect ratio.
  • ISO 536 – determination of grammage (g/m²) for paper and board.
  • ANSI/ASME Y14.1 – US sheet sizes (size A = 8.5 × 11 in).
  • Pound-to-gsm conversions are calculated from the basis sizes named above and the definition 1 lb = 453.59 g; manufacturers may round differently.
