Weekly planner

Plan your week on one page: a box for every day, space for notes and a to-do list that keeps your priorities in view.

Dated or undated?

Enter a start date and every box is labeled with the weekday and date, and the header shows the date range – handy if you print week by week. The planner begins with whatever day you enter: choose a Monday for a Monday-to-Sunday week, or a Sunday if your week starts on Sunday. Leave the date empty to get an undated page running Monday to Sunday that you can print in bulk and use whenever you need it, without wasting pages on weeks you skip. The title at the top is yours to change, for example to “Week 11” or “Project launch”.

What is on the page

  • Seven day boxes and a Notes box: in landscape they sit in two rows of four, on A4 about 49 × 80 mm each with ten writing lines. In portrait they form four rows of two.
  • To do this week: a 60 mm column with 22 check boxes in A4 landscape, or a strip along the bottom in portrait.
  • Weekend tint: Saturday and Sunday are lightly shaded so the work week stands out.
  • Writing lines: 6.5 mm apart; switch them off to draw time blocks or sketches.
  • Title and accent color: the title replaces “Weekly plan”, and the accent color is used for the header line and day labels.

How to plan the week

  1. Look back (5 minutes): what got done last week, what moves forward?
  2. Fixed appointments first: meetings, classes and family commitments go into the day boxes.
  3. Collect tasks in the to-do column.
  4. Pick three priorities and give each a day – a task with a slot gets done far more often than a task on an open list.
  5. Leave buffer on every day for the unexpected.

A filled example: a student's exam week

A student prints a dated page for an exam week. Exams go into the day boxes first, in red: statistics on Tuesday at 9:00, history on Thursday at 13:00. Revision blocks follow, written as times with the topic (“Mon 14–17: statistics, chapters 4–6”). The to-do column holds the small things – return library books, print the formula sheet, book a bike repair – each with a check box. The Notes box holds room numbers and the professor's office hours. On Friday evening, every finished revision block is ticked, and two topics that did not get enough time go straight onto next week's page. Saturday is deliberately left almost empty: one line says “rest, groceries”.

Without the to-do column, landscape boxes get wider (60.8 mm on US Letter) and portrait boxes taller, with up to seven lines. On A5 portrait, the to-do strip leaves only two lines per day, so either switch the to-do list off there or use landscape.

The writing lines inside the day boxes are 6.5 mm apart – slightly tighter than college ruling (about 7.1 mm) and a little wider than narrow ruling (6.35 mm). Most people can write one line of normal handwriting per line; if you write large, switch the lines off and use the box freely.

More ways to use the page

  • Hours log: freelancers write the hours per client in each day box and the weekly total into Notes, which makes invoicing quick at the end of the month.
  • Lesson plan: teachers use one box per school day with the periods as lines; the eighth box holds materials to prepare.
  • Shared household week: one page on the fridge, one color per person, and the to-do column for chores anyone can take.

Paper for a planner you write in daily

Weekly pages are written, crossed out and carried around for seven days. 90–100 g/m² paper keeps gel pens from showing through and stands up to a ring binder better than thin copy paper. If you print double-sided in portrait, choose “flip on long edge” so the back page is the right way up.

Printing tips

Landscape fills a clipboard or desk best and gives each day the most writing lines; portrait fits ring binders, especially in A5 or Half Letter. Print several undated pages at once and keep them with your monthly calendar; add a daily planner for the busiest days. All three share the same accent colors, so a printed set looks consistent.

Questions

Can I print several weeks at once?

Print the undated version as many times as you like, or create dated pages week by week by changing the start date.

Does the planner work in A5 binders?

Yes – choose A5 or Half Letter. Portrait orientation usually works best for small binders.

Can the week start on Sunday?

Yes. Enter a Sunday as the start date and the boxes run Sunday to Saturday with the correct dates. Undated pages start on Monday.

Can I remove the to-do list?

Untick “To-do column” and the day boxes use the full width or height of the page.

What is the eighth box for?

It is labeled Notes and holds everything that belongs to the week but not to a day: phone numbers, a shopping item, ideas or a quote for the week.

How many lines are in each day box?

On A4 landscape ten, on US Letter landscape eleven, and fewer on small or portrait pages – see the table above. The lines are 6.5 mm apart.

Which setting gives the most writing room per day?

US Letter or A4 in landscape with the to-do column switched off: each day box is then about 61–65 mm wide and 80 mm tall.
