Weekly planner

Plan your week on one page: a box for every day, space for notes and a to-do list that keeps your priorities in view.

Dated or undated?

Enter a start date and every box is labelled with the weekday and date – handy if you print week by week. Leave the date empty to get an undated planner that you can print in bulk and use whenever you need it, without wasting pages on weeks you skip.

A simple weekly planning routine

  1. Look back (5 minutes): what got done last week, what moves forward?
  2. Fixed appointments first: enter meetings, classes and family commitments.
  3. Pick three priorities: the tasks that would make the week a success. Put them on the to-do list.
  4. Give each priority a day: a task with a slot gets done far more often than a task on an open list.
  5. Leave buffer: keep some space free on each day for the unexpected.

Landscape or portrait?

In landscape, the days sit in two rows of four boxes with a tall to-do column on the right – the classic desk planner. Portrait stacks the days in four rows and moves the to-do list to the bottom, which fits ring binders and clipboards.

Weekends included

Saturday and Sunday get their own boxes, lightly tinted so the work week stands out. The eighth box is for notes – shopping, ideas, phone numbers or a quote for the week.

Combine with other pages

Many people use a monthly calendar for the overview, this weekly page for planning and a daily planner for busy days. All three share the same accent colours, so your printed set looks consistent.

Questions

Can I print several weeks at once?

Print the undated version as many times as you like, or create dated pages week by week by changing the start date.

Does the planner work in A5 binders?

Yes – choose A5 or Half Letter. Portrait orientation usually works best for small binders.
