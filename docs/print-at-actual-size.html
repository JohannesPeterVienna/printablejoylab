How to print at actual size (and why your grid comes out wrong)

A4 vs US Letter, “Fit to page” and the settings that keep 5 mm at 5 mm.

Reviewed November 3, 2026 · Printable Joy Lab editorial team

You download a 5 mm grid, print it, and the squares measure 4.7 mm. Or the last row of your monthly calendar disappears at the bottom of the sheet. In almost every case the PDF is fine and a single setting in the print dialog is responsible. This guide explains what happens in numbers, which option to pick, and how to check the result with a ruler.

Why printed grids come out too small

A PDF stores its page size exactly. When you print, the program compares that page with the sheet in the tray and with the area the printer can physically reach. If you have chosen an option such as Fit, Fit to page, Fit to printable area or Shrink oversized pages, the program shrinks the whole page until it fits inside that area. A 3–6% reduction is invisible on a letter but obvious on graph paper, dot grid or a calendar, where the spacing is the whole point of the page.

Two different problems usually hide behind “it prints wrong”:

  • Paper size mismatch: the PDF is A4 but the printer holds US Letter (or the other way round). The program either cuts off part of the page or scales it.
  • Scaling to the printable area: the sizes match, but the program still shrinks the page a little so that it fits inside the printer's unprintable border.

Both are solved in the same two steps: generate the template in the paper size you really have, and print at 100%.

A4 and US Letter in numbers

A4 belongs to the international A series defined in ISO 216. US Letter is the North American standard size, listed as size A in ANSI/ASME Y14.1. They look similar, but they are different shapes:

A4210 × 2978.27 × 11.69Most countries outside North America

A5148 × 2105.83 × 8.27Half of A4; common planner and notebook size

US Letter is 5.9 mm wider than A4 but 17.6 mm shorter. That height difference is why an A4 calendar printed on Letter paper loses its bottom edge, or gets shrunk. Every template on this site lets you choose A4, US Letter, A5 or Half Letter, so the PDF can match the sheet before you even open the print dialog.

What “Fit” does to your page

Scaling is simple arithmetic: the program divides the available width and height by the page width and height and uses the smaller ratio. The table shows typical results for a page that contains a 5 mm grid.

SituationScale5 mm grid prints as10 cm measures
Matching paper, printed at 100% / Actual size100%5.00 mm10.0 cm
A4 PDF fitted onto US Letter (279.4 ÷ 297)about 94%about 4.70 mmabout 9.4 cm
US Letter PDF fitted onto A4 (210 ÷ 215.9)about 97%about 4.86 mmabout 9.7 cm
Matching A4, fitted inside a 4 mm border on every side (202 ÷ 210)about 96%about 4.81 mmabout 9.6 cm

The last row is the sneaky one: even with the correct paper, “Fit to printable area” shrinks the page because the printer cannot reach the outermost few millimeters. The real border depends on the printer model.

Where to find the setting

Program menus change between versions, but the wording is usually close to the following. The goal is always the same: 100%, Actual size, Default or None – never a “fit” or “shrink” option.

  • Chrome and Edge (Windows, Mac, ChromeOS): open More settings and look at Scale. Choose Default, Actual size or Custom with 100. Avoid Fit to page or Fit to printable area. Also check that Paper size matches the PDF.
  • Preview on a Mac: in the print dialog, set Scale to 100% and make sure Scale to Fit is not selected. Check the paper size in the same dialog.
  • Adobe Acrobat Reader: under Page Sizing & Handling, choose Actual size. Both Fit and Shrink oversized pages can reduce a full-size page.
  • Firefox: under Scale, pick 100 instead of Fit to page width.
  • iPhone and iPad (AirPrint): there is usually no scale control. Generate the PDF in the size that is loaded in the printer, then choose the matching paper size in the print options if they are offered.
  • Android: in the print preview, select the paper size first; if the printer plugin offers a scaling or “fit” option, switch it off or set it to 100%.

Why every template keeps a white border

Most home inkjet and laser printers cannot print to the very edge of the sheet; a border of roughly 3–6 mm stays white, depending on the model. Our templates keep 12 mm free at the top and sides on A4 and US Letter and 9 mm on A5 and Half Letter. At the bottom the border is 5 mm larger, because the optional site credit sits there. Because nothing important lies in the outer few millimeters, you can print at 100% without losing a line – there is no need to let the printer shrink anything.

Worked example: checking a print with a ruler

Suppose you want to be sure your 5 mm dot grid really prints at 5 mm before you print a 50-sheet stack.

  1. Open the graph paper template, choose your paper size, set the grid to 1 cm and switch bold lines off.
  2. Print one page with the scale set to 100% (see the list above).
  3. Lay a ruler along one row and count ten squares. Then do the same down a column, because width and height can be scaled separately when the paper size is wrong.
  4. Compare with the table: 10.0 cm means you are done; about 9.4 cm means an A4 page went onto Letter paper with “Fit”; about 9.7 cm means the opposite; 9.5–9.6 cm on matching paper means a “fit to printable area” option is still active.
  5. Fix the setting, print once more and measure again. Only then print the rest of the stack in the pattern you actually wanted.

A difference of half a millimeter over 10 cm is hard to see on the finished page and usually not worth chasing; a difference of several millimeters is always a scaling setting.

Small pages, booklets and two-sided printing

If you want A5 pages, the cleanest result is to create the A5 version of the template and print it on A5 paper. Printing an A4 page “2 pages per sheet” also works, but the printer then reduces each page to about 71% (1 ÷ √2) – a 5 mm grid becomes roughly 3.5 mm. For which paper weight to load, see our paper guide, which has a gsm to pound conversion table.

FAQ

Why is the bottom of my calendar missing?

The PDF is taller than the sheet, which happens when an A4 page is printed on US Letter without scaling. Generate the calendar in US Letter instead, or print the A4 version on A4 paper.

Is “Default” the same as 100%?

In most browsers, yes: Default prints the page at its stored size. If a ruler check shows a reduction, choose Custom and type 100.

Can I print edge to edge?

Only some printers support borderless printing, mostly for photo paper. It is not needed here, because the templates already keep a safe border.

Sources and standards

  • ISO 216 – international paper sizes of the A and B series, including A4 (210 × 297 mm) and A5 (148 × 210 mm).
  • ANSI/ASME Y14.1 – US drawing sheet sizes; size A is 8.5 × 11 in, the same as US Letter.
  • Conversions use the exact definition 1 inch = 25.4 mm. Scaling figures are calculated from these dimensions; print dialog names reflect common program versions at the time of review.
