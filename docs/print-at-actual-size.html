How to print at actual size (and why your grid comes out wrong)

A4 vs US Letter, “Fit to page” and the settings that keep 5 mm at 5 mm.

You download a 5 mm grid, print it – and it measures 4.8 mm. Or the bottom line of your calendar is cut off. Almost always, the reason is a single print setting. Here is how to get exact results.

A4 and US Letter are different shapes

A4 (210 × 297 mm) is the standard almost everywhere outside North America. US Letter (8.5 × 11 inches, 215.9 × 279.4 mm) is slightly wider and noticeably shorter. If you print an A4 file on Letter paper, the printer either cuts off the bottom or shrinks the whole page to fit. That is why every template here lets you choose the paper size you actually have.

The one setting that matters: scale

In the print dialog, look for Scale, Page sizing or Fit. Choose 100 %, Actual size or None – not “Fit to page” or “Shrink oversized pages”. Fit-to-page shrinks the content by a few percent to make room for the printer's margins, which is invisible on a letter but obvious on graph paper.

  • Windows (Edge/Chrome): More settings → Scale → Default or 100.
  • Mac (Preview): Scale 100 %, untick “Scale to fit”.
  • Adobe Acrobat Reader: Page Sizing & Handling → Actual size.
  • iPhone/iPad: AirPrint uses the paper size of the file; make sure it matches the paper in the printer.
  • Android: In the print preview, set the paper size, then open the menu and choose 100 % if offered.

Why our pages leave a margin

Most home printers cannot print right to the edge of the sheet – they need 3–6 mm of unprinted border. Our templates keep at least 9–12 mm free on every side, so nothing is cut off and you can still print at 100 %.

Check with a ruler

Print a page of graph paper with a 1 cm grid and measure ten squares: they should be exactly 10 cm. If they measure 9.6 cm, your printer is still scaling.

Paper weight

Standard copy paper (80 g/m² or 20 lb) is fine for most pages. For planners you write on with markers, 100–120 g/m² paper reduces bleed-through and feels more like a notebook.
