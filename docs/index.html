Choose a sheet by what you need to put on it

For a walking route with turns on either side of the start, choose graph paper and turn on coordinate axes. A 5 mm square can stand for one city block. For a sketch of a box, choose isometric paper so its three edge directions already run through the dots.

For a rotating work shift, open the weekly planner and write the fixed hours first. If a single shift needs appointments at half-hour intervals, give that day a separate time grid. For tasks you carry between days, print one undated checklist instead.

Made for real printers

Every template is built in the paper size you choose – A4, US Letter, A5 or Half Letter – so nothing has to be shrunk to fit. Grids, boxes and text stay at least 12 mm from the edge (9 mm on the two small sizes), more than the unprintable border of typical home printers; only the lines of lined paper deliberately run from edge to edge, and the small optional site credit sits in the bottom margin. The Print button sends the exact page size to your browser with no extra margins, and the PDF stores it too. Scale is the one setting to watch: print at 100% or “Actual size”, as our guide to printing at actual size explains, and a 5 mm grid really measures 5 mm. To check your own printer, print the test page with its 100 mm square and edge rulers; the paper size reference calculates what any “Fit” setting does to a page.
