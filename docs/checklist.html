Checklist & to-do list

From packing lists to project steps: give your list a title, choose the layout and pre-fill items if you want.

A checklist for every occasion

Checklists are one of the simplest tools for doing things reliably. Aviation and hospitals use them so that routine steps are not skipped under pressure; at home they stop you from leaving the charger behind or forgetting to cancel the newspaper before a move. The value of a printed list is that it is complete before you start and visible while you work – you do not need to remember what is next, only to tick what is done. This generator makes one page with your title, 5 to 60 lines in one or two columns, and an optional priority mark on every line.

How to build your list

  1. Give it a title, such as “Camping trip” or “Move – week before”.
  2. Choose the number of lines. Fewer lines get more space: 25 lines on A4 are about 10 mm apart; 60 lines in two columns about 8 mm.
  3. Pick square boxes, round boxes or numbers.
  4. Optionally type items into the box, one per line, and they are printed on the lines – up to about 60 characters each.
  5. Tick “Priority column” to add three small dots at the end of each line; fill one, two or three to rank items.

Squares, circles or numbers?

Squares are the classic tick box and the easiest to see from a distance. Circles look softer and match bullet-journal pages. Numbered lines suit step-by-step instructions and ranked lists, where the order matters – a recipe, a setup procedure or your top ten books of the year.

A filled example: a packing list you reuse

A family types its standard packing list once: passports, chargers, medication, toothbrushes, swimwear, rain jackets, snacks for the car, and so on – 34 items. With two columns and 40 lines, the page has six empty lines at the end for trip-specific items. Before each trip they print a fresh copy, add “ski gloves” or “beach tent” by hand, and tick items as they go into the bags. Because the settings are remembered in the browser (or saved as a share link), next year's list is ready in seconds.

More ideas

  • Moving checklist: utilities, address changes, keys, meter readings.
  • Cleaning routine: a numbered list of weekly tasks in the order you do them.
  • Shopping list: pre-fill the items you buy every week; the meal planner has a category list for the rest.
  • Project steps: renovations, events or launches, one action per line.

Write better list items

Start each item with a verb – “Call the plumber”, not “Plumber”. Split anything that takes more than an hour into steps. Keep a single list per purpose instead of one endless list, and print a fresh one when more than half is ticked.

The spacing never exceeds 10 mm, so short lists do not stretch over the whole page. On A5 and Half Letter, 25 or more lines end up between 5.5 and 7 mm apart – fine for a shopping list, tight for longer items. For pre-filled items in two columns, keep each item to roughly 50 characters on A4 or Letter and about 30 on A5; longer items run into the next column.

Read-do or do-confirm?

Checklists used in aviation and surgery come in two types, a distinction popularized by the surgeon Atul Gawande in The Checklist Manifesto. A read-do list is followed step by step as you work, like a recipe or a setup procedure – the numbered style fits it best. A do-confirm list is checked after you have done the work from memory, like a packing list you run through before leaving the house – square boxes fit it best. Gawande recommends keeping each checklist short, at about five to nine items per pause point, and concentrating on the steps that are most often missed rather than listing everything.

Paper for lists that get handled

A list that lives in a bag or on a clipboard needs more than copy paper: 120 g/m² or more does not wrinkle as quickly. For a reusable list in a sheet protector, print on 160–200 g/m² card stock so it stays stiff, and tick the boxes with a wet-erase marker. A list printed in grayscale loses nothing, because the boxes and lines are gray anyway.

Printing tips

The page is always portrait. For lists you laminate and reuse with a dry-erase marker, choose large spacing and bold square boxes, and print on heavier paper before laminating. A5 and Half Letter make handy pocket lists for shopping. For a weekly to-do list tied to dates, use the weekly planner; for daily routines tracked over a month, the habit tracker.

Questions

How many items fit on one page?

Up to 60 lines in two columns. With fewer lines, the spacing grows so the page still looks balanced.

Can I save my pre-filled list?

Your last settings are remembered in this browser. You can also copy a share link that contains your list.

Can I print a list with two columns of different topics?

Choose two columns and pre-fill the items in order: the first half of your lines fills the left column, the rest the right column. Leave empty lines to separate topics.

Can I make a numbered list without check boxes?

Yes. Choose “Numbered” as the checkbox style and every line gets a number instead of a box.

Do long pre-filled items fit in two columns?

Roughly 50 characters fit in a column on A4 or US Letter and about 30 on A5. Longer items run into the next column, so shorten them or use one column.

Which checkbox style should I use for step-by-step instructions?

Numbered. When the order matters – a recipe, a setup, a closing routine – numbers make it easy to see where you stopped.
